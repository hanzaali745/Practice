# Behavioral interviews — your story bank

Every DevOps loop has a behavioral part: "tell me about a time...". Interviewers check how you work with people,
handle failure and take ownership. Prepare **stories**, not answers.

## STAR, in two minutes
| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **S**ituation | 1–2 sentences of context | "Our deploys took a day and broke often." |
| **T**ask | your responsibility | "I owned making deploys safe and fast." |
| **A**ction | what **you** did — the longest part, specific, "I" not "we" | "I built a pipeline that…, convinced the team by…" |
| **R**esult | measurable outcome + what you learned | "Deploys went from 1/week to 5/day; I learned to…" |

No job experience yet? Use the bootcamp — it's real work. Your capstones, incidents and postmortems are legitimate
stories: say clearly that they were projects, and go deep on the technical decisions.

## Common questions (practise out loud)
1. Tell me about yourself.
2. Why DevOps? Why this company?
3. Tell me about a production incident. What did you learn?
4. Describe a time you made a mistake.
5. How do you handle being on call?
6. Tell me about a time you improved a process.
7. How do you stay up to date?
8. Describe a conflict with a colleague and how you resolved it.
9. What would you do in your first 90 days here?
10. Where do you want to be in 3 years?

## Questions to ask THEM (always have 3 ready)
- What does on-call look like here — how often, how many pages a week, is it paid?
- How do changes get to production? How often do you deploy?
- What's the biggest reliability or infrastructure problem the team has right now?
- How do you run postmortems?
- What would success look like for this role after 6 months?
- How do engineers learn here (budget, time, certifications)?
